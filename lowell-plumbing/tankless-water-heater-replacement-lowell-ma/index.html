    Picking a Tankless for Your Lowell, MA Home

    Tank to Tankless Conversion in Lowell, MA

    Converting from a tank to tankless takes a full day in most homes. Expect a bigger gas line, a new vent run and a wall-mounted spot for the heater. We cap or remove the old flue.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Lowell

    Sizing a tankless starts with how many fixtures run at once and how cold the incoming water is, not the square footage of the house. Two showers and a dishwasher at the same time can need 7 or 8 gallons per minute. That number picks the unit. We handle the gas, water and vent changes needed to bring the old installation up to current code.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Lowell

    Rinnai units we pull are usually 12 to 18 years old, and the failure tends to be a leaking heat exchanger or a dead control board. Newer Rinnai models usually fit the old mounting pattern. That keeps wall patching small. We match the vent type or convert to PVC on a condensing model.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Lowell, MA

    Pulling a 50 gallon tank out of a garage frees up floor space and ends standby heat loss. The catch is gas, since most tankless units need a 3/4-inch line or bigger plus new venting. We check the meter size and pipe run before quoting, and we cap or reroute the old flue and seal the roof penetration if it is no longer needed. Plan on a full day.

    Like-for-Like Tankless Swaps in Lowell

    A cracked heat exchanger or a failed control board on an older tankless is usually not worth repairing past the ten-year mark. We match the new unit to your gas line, vent type and mounting spot whenever we can. Most swaps take four to six hours.

    Point-of-Use Electric Tankless in Lowell

    Point-of-use electric tankless heaters under a sink are a different animal from whole-house units. A small under-sink unit needs one 20 or 30 amp circuit and suits a distant bath or break room. Most take under two hours.

    Condensing Tankless Replacement in Lowell

    Condensing tankless units like the Navien NPE or Rinnai Sensei pass 90 percent efficiency by pulling extra heat out of the exhaust. The vent is plain PVC. The condensate is acidic, so we install a neutralizer cartridge and run the line to an approved drain. Humid summers bring condensation on cold water lines and musty basements, which can hide slow leaks until damage appears. Showers and laundry run all year, so the gas savings do too.

    Commercial Tankless Replacement in Lowell

    Cascade controls on a commercial rack decide which unit fires first, and after one unit is replaced they need rebalancing. Otherwise one unit does all the work. Descaling gets scheduled by usage.
