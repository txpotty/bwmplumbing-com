    Cured-In-Place Pipe Prep and Lining in Lowell

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. A poor prep job means a wrinkled liner. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Pipe Bursting in Lowell, MA

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. Before pulling, the crew heat-fuses sections of HDPE into one continuous length on the ground. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. A 60-foot pull often takes under an hour.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Lining in Lowell, MA

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. Plumbers call that channel rot, a groove that eats through the bottom. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Relining in Lowell, MA

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. The crew saturates a felt tube with two-part epoxy on a roller table at the truck, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Ambient cure takes a few hours, and hot water or steam shortens it. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.
