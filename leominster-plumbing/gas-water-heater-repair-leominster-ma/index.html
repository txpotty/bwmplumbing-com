    Propane Water Heater Repair in Leominster

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Bradford White & Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Leominster, MA

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Leominster, MA. Rheem gas models mostly use White-Rodgers or Honeywell controls with a status light. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. We keep both on the truck. Using factory parts keeps your tank warranty valid.

    Pilot Light Repair in Leominster

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.

    Pilot Light and Burner Issues in Leominster

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.
