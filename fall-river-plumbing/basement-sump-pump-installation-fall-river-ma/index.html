    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Fall River, MA

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. Snowmelt combined with rain is a classic recipe for wet basements, overworked sump pumps and municipal sewer backups. We add a second pump in the same pit with its own float, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak.     Pedestal Sump Pumps in Fall River

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Fall River

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in Fall River. The low end covers a pump swap in an existing pit, while a new pit cut through the slab, a long discharge run, or a battery backup pushes the total toward the top of that range. You get a written price after we look.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. A new pit with slab cutting can stretch it to a full day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. We tell you up front.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Local plumbing inspectors apply the state code consistently, so permitted work is expected to be done to the letter. New pits, new circuits and new discharge routes are the parts most likely to need a plumbing permit. The local requirements get checked before we start, and when a permit applies we pull it and schedule the inspection ourselves. No trips to the permit office.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Other signs are rust on the housing and rapid cycling, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. After about ten years, plan on replacing it.

    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Pour a five gallon bucket of water into the pit, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. That is the whole test.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. Rust stains and a float that sticks are warning signs too, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. The job combines electrical, plumbing and concrete work, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Errors appear at the worst moment.
