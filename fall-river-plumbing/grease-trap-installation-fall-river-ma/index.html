    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Fall River

    Expect roughly $1,500 to $4,000 for a small indoor trap and $7,000 to $20,000 or more for an outdoor gravity interceptor. A floor-recessed trap costs more than one set on the floor. Location drives the rest. Lids under traffic cost more, and so does repaving.

    We start with a fixture list and the drain rate of each fixture. Sinks are calculated by compartment volume and drain-down time, typically one or two minutes, and dish machines by the manufacturer's discharge rating. That gives the GPM figure. Hydromechanical traps are picked by that GPM rating, while gravity interceptors are sized by volume and retention time from the same data.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. For a small indoor trap in a busy kitchen, that can be weekly. Outdoor tanks go longer. A gravity interceptor typically gets pumped monthly to quarterly, and some sewer programs set the schedule for you.

    It keeps cooking grease out of the public sewer. Hot greasy water cools as it moves through the trap, the grease floats to the top, and cleaner water leaves through the outlet. Without one, that grease hardens in the city mains and causes sewer overflows, and sewer authorities fine the kitchens they trace it to. Your own drain lines benefit too.

    On a commercial kitchen, no. Nearly every jurisdiction requires a licensed plumber to pull the permit and do the work. For a home kitchen you might be allowed to, as long as the flow control and venting are done correctly. Ask the building department first.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. We register the unit with the maker so the factory warranty is on record. Keep your cleaning logs. Some manufacturers ask for proof of regular service before they honor a claim on a cracked body or a failed baffle.

    Watch the pump-out bills. Rising service frequency means the trap has been outgrown or the baffles have failed. Smell is another clue. Slow floor sinks, grease odor at the trap, a lid that no longer seals, or a failed FOG inspection all point the same way, and we can tell you which it is in one visit.
