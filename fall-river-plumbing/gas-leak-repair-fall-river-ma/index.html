    Permitting and Code Compliance in Fall River, MA

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. We tell you up front which way your job falls. The fee goes on the estimate. The work follows Massachusetts' adopted fuel gas code and any local amendments. The signed inspection card goes to you, and a future buyer's inspector will likely ask to see it.

    Outdoor Gas Line Repair in Fall River

    Grills, fire pits, pool heaters and patio heaters often sit at the end of a long buried line that nobody has looked at since it went in. Fence posts and sprinkler trenches hit those lines more often than corrosion does. Dead grass is a tell. We pressure test the outdoor branch on its own, locate the leak and replace the run with polyethylene and a tracer wire. Above-ground stub-ups get a new riser and a shutoff valve.

    Appliance Connector Replacement in Fall River

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. If your range or water heater still has one, it should come out. We see them weekly. The replacement is a coated stainless connector sized for the appliance, with a new ball valve and a sediment trap where one is required. Bubble testing follows before the burner is lit.

    Emergency Gas Leak Repair in Fall River, MA

    Hissing at the meter, a sulfur smell in the kitchen, a dead patch of lawn or a carbon monoxide alarm all mean the same thing: get everyone outside before you touch anything. From the yard or a neighbor's porch, call the gas utility first. BWM Plumbing runs emergency gas calls in Fall River, MA around the clock. After the utility closes the meter, we cut off the bad branch, fix it and test it. Unaffected appliances usually come back the same day.

    Commercial Gas Leak Repair in Fall River

    Commercial gas piping often runs at 2 psi or higher with regulators at each appliance bank, so a leak test there is not the same as a house test. We check regulator lockup and vent lines along with the joints. Rooftop units leak too. Where downtime costs you money, we isolate only the affected branch and keep the rest of the building running. Staff get a short walkthrough on the shutoff locations.

    Underground Gas Line Repair in Fall River

    Yard lines run from the meter to the house, and often on to a detached garage, a pool heater, a grill or an outdoor kitchen. Buried steel loses its coating to rocks in the backfill and then rusts through. Roots and settling do damage too. We call 811 for locates, dig at the suspected spot and replace the bad run with yellow polyethylene pipe and tracer wire. Sand goes around the new pipe before backfill, per Fall River trench rules.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Fall River, MA

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Connectors get bent every time a range is pulled out to clean behind it. Valves weep at the stem. We swap in a new coated stainless connector, add a quarter-turn ball valve and set a sediment trap where the code calls for it. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is relit.

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Fall River, MA

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. Sizing starts with the total BTU load and the longest run from the meter. Undersized pipe starves burners. Old pipe with heavy rust or bad threads gets replaced instead of patched, usually with black iron or CSST depending on access. A big tankless unit may also need a bigger meter from the utility.
