    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Newton

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in Newton usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Freeze-proofing is cheaper. Insulation, heat cable and frost-free faucets usually cost less than one burst repair.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Visible frost on a pipe confirms it. You may also notice a toilet that will not refill, a bulge in a pipe, or a strange smell from a drain, and any of those means you should open the faucet and call before the line thaws on its own.

    For an exposed pipe you can reach, yes, carefully. Open the faucet first. Warm the pipe with a hair dryer or a space heater kept a safe distance away, working from the faucet back toward the frozen section, and never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame near framing.

    Pipe location matters more than brand. Metal pipe cracks more easily. Metal pipe in cold spots splits most, and PEX often survives except at the fittings.

    Keep cold air away from the pipe. Seal and insulate around exposed lines. Close crawlspace vents for the season, insulate attic and garage runs, swap old hose bibs for frost-free models, and add self-regulating heat cable on any run that still gets too cold despite insulation.

    A short, exposed section usually thaws in 30 to 60 minutes. Hidden lines take longer. Lines in walls or plastic pipe can take a few hours with gentler heat.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. Water drains out of the stem after shutoff. Leaving a hose attached defeats it, since trapped water can split the valve inside the wall.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Bigger work might. Repiping a section, rerouting a line into a heated wall, or replacing a backflow preventer may require a permit in many jurisdictions, and we pull it when needed so the work is on record.
