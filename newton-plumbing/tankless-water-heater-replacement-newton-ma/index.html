    Picking a Tankless for Your Newton, MA House

    Whole-House Electric Tankless Replacement in Newton

    Whole-house electric tankless performance drops when the incoming water is cold, since the unit has to raise the temperature further at the same amperage. We replace Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart units. Sometimes a heat pump tank serves the house better, and we will say so.

    Tankless-to-Tankless Replacement in Newton

    Replacing an existing tankless with a new one is usually the simpler job, since the gas line, vent path and water connections are already there. We still check sizing against the new model. Old service valves often get replaced too.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Newton

    Sizing a tankless starts with how many fixtures run at once and how cold the incoming water is, not the square footage of the house. Run two showers and the dishwasher together and you are near 8 GPM. The unit is chosen from that figure. Any gas, water or vent changes the new unit needs get done to current code.

    Leaking Tankless Replacement in Newton

    A leaking tankless can drip inside the cabinet for weeks before anyone notices the puddle. Shut the gas and cold isolation valve now. Then we look at whether the leak is a fitting or the heat exchanger itself, because one is a $20 fix and the other means a new unit.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Newton, MA

    Navien NPE units are condensing, and the A models have a built-in recirculation pump and buffer tank that cut down on cold-water sandwiches. Their combi boilers handle both space heat and domestic water. We set up both.

    Like-for-Like Tankless Swaps in Newton

    A cracked heat exchanger or a failed control board on an older tankless is usually not worth repairing past the ten-year mark. The replacement gets matched to the existing gas, vent and mount where possible. Plan on four to six hours.

    Tank to Tankless Conversion in Newton, MA

    Converting from a tank to tankless takes a full day in most homes. The work usually includes a larger gas line, new venting through a wall or roof, and a new spot on the wall for the unit. The old flue gets capped.

    Electric tankless water heaters skip the gas line entirely, but a whole-house model can need 120 to 160 amps on two to four double-pole breakers. Plenty of panels do not have room. Panel size and wire gauge get checked first.
