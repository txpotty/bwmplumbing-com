    Gas Line Relocation and Upgrades in Medford, MA

    A kitchen remodel or a new tankless water heater can push a gas system past what its original pipe can deliver. Tankless units need a lot of gas. A whole-house tankless heater can draw 150,000 BTU or more, and that often means upsizing the gas line all the way back toward the meter. Load calculation comes before any pipe. Then we size the new piping so every appliance in the house still gets full pressure at the same time.

    CSST Repair and Bonding in Medford, MA

    Newer homes often have corrugated stainless steel tubing running through the attic. CSST can be damaged by nails, rodents or lightning, and older yellow-jacket CSST needs proper bonding to the electrical grounding system to reduce the risk of a lightning arc. Bonding is a short job. Damaged runs get spliced with the manufacturer's fittings, and we confirm the bond on the way out. Nor'easters and heavy spring rain can raise groundwater quickly in Massachusetts, sending water into basements that stay dry most of the year.

    Gas Leak Repair at Fittings and Valves in Medford

    Most residential gas leaks are at a threaded joint, a valve stem or a flexible connector, not in the middle of a pipe. That keeps repairs small. We back out the leaking joint, clean the threads, apply gas-rated thread sealant and reassemble it, or replace the fitting or valve if the threads are damaged. We soap-test the repaired joint and pressure test the system before leaving.

    Gas Appliance Connector Repair in Medford, MA

    Weak flames or a smell near one appliance often point to the connector or shutoff behind it. Uncoated brass connectors from decades ago crack at the flare fittings and should be replaced on sight. We replace them with coated stainless connectors. Each appliance also gets a shutoff you can reach and a sediment trap where required, and we check that the connector length and diameter match the appliance rating.

    Propane Gas Line Service in Medford, MA

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. We repair buried and above-ground propane lines, replace leaking regulators and fittings and check that each appliance has been converted with the correct orifices for propane. A natural gas burner running on propane will burn rich and soot up. We test the system the same way we test natural gas piping.
