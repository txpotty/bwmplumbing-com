    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Medford

    Prices in Medford, MA usually land between $800 and $2,500. A pedestal unit runs cheaper than a cast iron submersible, and the biggest swing comes from whether we have to excavate a new pit and whether you want a battery backup added to the same basin. Every quote is itemized.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Visible rust and a stuck float are other red flags, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. The job combines electrical, plumbing and concrete work, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Mistakes show up in storms.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    Coastal communities also deal with storm surge and tidal flooding, which can affect drains and septic systems near the water. Outages and storms arrive together, so we recommend a battery backup in most basements. The backup starts on its own if the main pump has no power, and it also catches the water if the primary switch sticks or the pump simply cannot keep up with a heavy inflow. It is cheap insurance.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. All four have parts at local supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. We stock the common models.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. A pedestal mounts the motor on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Both move water fine.
