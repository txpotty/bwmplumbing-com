    Why Choose BWM Plumbing for Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lynn, MA?

    Ejector pumps handle raw sewage, so a sloppy install shows up as odors, backups and expensive cleanup. We seal the lid with gaskets at every penetration, vent the basin through the roof and install a check valve so waste in the discharge line cannot drain back into the pit after each cycle. Sewer gas stays out.

    • Alarm Protection, A high water alarm warns you before a backup.
    • Code Compliance, Sealed lid, roof vent and check valve on each install.

    Citywide Coverage

    Homes all over Lynn have ejector systems we installed, from older houses with a basement toilet to new builds with full lower-level suites. Each one gets a basin location, vent path and discharge route planned around its own layout before we quote a price, because two houses on the same street can drain very differently. No two jobs match.

    Experienced Team

    The team understands basin sizing, venting rules and discharge routing to the building drain. They line up the dedicated pump circuit and alarm power with the electrician, because a pump on a shared circuit can trip a breaker and leave the basin filling. Each pump gets its own breaker.

    Safety-First Approach

    Ejector pumps sit in water below grade, so electrical safety matters. We make sure the pump is on its own properly grounded circuit with the protection code requires, and that the alarm has a separate power source so a tripped pump breaker does not also silence the alarm. Two circuits, not one.

    Grinder Pump Installation in Lynn

    Grinder pumps chop waste into a fine slurry first, which lets them push through small pipe over long distances. A grinder makes sense when the sewer is distant, uphill or pressurized, such as a pressure sewer where each home pumps into a shared main. Grinders run pricier. A standard ejector often covers a basement bath.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Lynn

    Digging the pit means cutting the slab and excavating below it, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Rocky glacial soil and shallow ledge are common in Massachusetts, which can make digging for sewer and water lines slower and more expensive. The basin sits on gravel with backfill around it so rising groundwater cannot float it, and new concrete seals the floor and locks it down. A floating basin breaks pipe.

    Liberty Pumps Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lynn, MA

    Liberty builds simplex and duplex packages that arrive with the basin, pump, lid and fittings matched. That speeds up new installs, and their wide-angle float switches are less likely to hang up on the basin wall than older tethered floats, which is one of the most common reasons a pump fails to start. Switch failures cause many calls.

    Ejector Pump Replacement in Lynn

    Ejector pumps tend to run 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed. Watch for constant running, a hum or grind, repeated alarms and odor at the pit, since any of them means trouble is close. Old switches fail first.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Lynn, MA

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes often need duplex systems with an alternating control panel. The panel switches the lead pump each cycle so wear is shared, and if the lead pump fails or the flow is heavy, the second pump starts automatically and the panel trips an alarm so someone knows to call for service. Redundancy matters here.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Lynn, MA

    The basin is where the system lives, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. A larger basin means longer, less frequent cycles that spare the motor and switch, so we go bigger when the pit takes a laundry or several fixtures. Lids must be gas-tight.

    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lynn

    Zoeller's sewage line runs from light residential to heavy duty, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. The model comes from the pump curve for your lift, and most basement baths need a four-tenths or half horsepower unit that passes two-inch solids. Bigger lifts need more.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Lynn

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so the toilet, shower and sink drain to a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We cut the slab, trench the drain lines to the basin, set it, run a two-inch vent up through the house to the roof and pipe the discharge to the building drain with a check valve and shutoff. The slab gets repoured.
