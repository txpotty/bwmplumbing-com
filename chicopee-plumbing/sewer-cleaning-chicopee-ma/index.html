    Main Sewer Line Clearing in Chicopee

    The main carries everything out of the house. A blockage there stops every drain.     Cast Iron Sewer Cleaning in Chicopee, MA

    Cast iron rots from the inside. Cast iron drains, galvanized supply lines and old lead service connections are still common in multi-family houses and older single-family homes. Scale builds on top while the bottom channels out. Because a heavy cutter can punch through weak iron, we use chain descalers and a lower-pressure jet, then check the wall thickness on camera.

    Apartment and Strip Center Sewers in Chicopee, MA

    Shared laterals carry heavy loads. Many tenants use a single line.     Hydro Jetting for Sewer Lines in Chicopee

    Jetting cleans the whole pipe wall. A cable only makes a path. A jetter sends high-pressure water around the full circumference of the pipe, removing buildup that a cable would leave behind.

    Emergency Sewer Backup Cleaning in Chicopee

    Sewage coming up through a shower or floor drain? Shut off the washer and dishwasher. We send a tech with a main line machine, protective gear and a wet vac, and the first priority is getting the line open so wastewater can leave the house, then we disinfect the area and take photos for an insurance claim.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Lines in Chicopee

    Older properties may still have clay or Orangeburg pipe. Both have weak spots. Clay joints invite roots, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid-1900s, flattens under the weight of the soil over time, so we clean these lines carefully and map the joints and soft spots on camera.

    Scheduled Sewer Cleaning in Chicopee, MA

    History predicts the next backup. We set a cleaning interval. A line that has backed up before can be cleaned on a set schedule, often every year or two, so it never stops again without warning.

    Grease and Scale Buildup Cleaning in Chicopee, MA

    Grease bonds with mineral scale into a hard crust. Pinhole leaks in older copper lines are a familiar problem where the water is aggressive, and they tend to show up in clusters. Over years the opening shrinks. Cabling opens a path, while jetting strips the crust off the walls so the line stays open longer, and the method depends on pipe material and condition.
