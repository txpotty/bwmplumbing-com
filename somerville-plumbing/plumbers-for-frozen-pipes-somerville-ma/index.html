    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Somerville

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in Somerville usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Freeze-proofing is cheaper. Insulating a crawlspace, adding heat cable to the worst run, and swapping outdoor faucets for frost-free models usually costs a few hundred dollars, which is less than a single burst pipe repair.

    Pipe location matters more than brand. Copper and galvanized split first. Metal pipe in cold spots splits most, and PEX often survives except at the fittings.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Visible frost on a pipe confirms it. A toilet that will not refill or a bulging pipe also points to ice.

    No water at one faucet after a cold night is the classic sign. Frost on the pipe confirms it. Feel the pipe where it runs through a cold space, and if one section is much colder than the rest or you can see a bulge, that is likely where the ice is sitting.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. The outside part drains when you close it. It only works if the hose is disconnected, because a connected hose traps water in the stem where it can freeze and split the valve inside the wall, which you may not notice until spring.

    Ice can split a pipe while frozen, but the leak only appears once the ice melts and water flows again. The crack was there all along. We pressure test every thawed line before calling the job done, and you should keep an eye on ceilings and walls near the frozen section for the next day or two.

    Open the faucet on that line and leave it open. It gives melting ice somewhere to go. Shut off the main, warm the area safely, and call us to thaw and test the line.

    No pipe is freeze-proof. PEX tolerates freezing better than most. It can stretch slightly as ice forms instead of splitting the way copper does, but the fittings at each end can still crack, so location and insulation matter more than the pipe itself.
