    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Replacement in Revere

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s. Over time it softens, goes oval and blisters on the inside, so a liner has nothing round to bond to and bursting is usually the better fix. Clay is different. Vitrified clay that still holds shape lines well.

    Cost Comparison of Trenchless vs Traditional in Revere, MA

    Depth changes the math. A line two feet down in open lawn is often cheaper to dig than to line, but a lateral eight feet deep that runs under the driveway and a porch costs far more to excavate than to burst or line from two pits. We quote both ways. Then you pick.

    Sectional Point Repair in Revere

    A point repair is a short liner, often 3 to 5 feet long, set over one bad spot. It rides in on an inflatable packer, guided by the camera footage, and the packer inflates to press the resin-soaked sleeve against the pipe wall until it cures. It fixes a single crack or root entry, not a whole failing line. Sound lines with one bad joint are the right candidates.

    Pipe Bursting in Revere, MA

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. The new HDPE pipe is butt-fused into one length first. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. It goes quickly.

    Perma-Liner and NuFlow Trenchless Systems in Revere

    Perma-Liner and NuFlow both make lining systems we use, each with its own resin chemistry and installation equipment. Both systems are made to meet the ASTM standards written for cured-in-place pipe. The brand matters less than the prep, because a liner bonded to a dirty, scaly pipe wall will not hold the way the manufacturer rates it. We clean to bare pipe first.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacements in Revere

    A bursting head is a steel cone, slightly wider than the old pipe, pulled through the line by a hydraulic unit or a winch cable. It cracks the old pipe outward into the soil while a string of new HDPE pipe follows right behind it. Fused joints, no gaps. This is the fix for collapsed pipe.
