    Which Sump Pump Fits Your Revere, MA Home

    Yard Drainage Pit Pumps in Revere, MA

    Yard drainage pits and window well pumps can sit dry from fall to spring and then seize the first time a big storm fills them. We clean the debris out and install a pump built to handle grit. We route the outlet away from the house. Test them each spring.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Revere

    Nor'easters and heavy spring rain can raise groundwater quickly in Massachusetts, sending water into basements that stay dry most of the year. A standard pump just sits there once the power drops. We add a 12-volt backup pump on its own float, mounted a few inches above the primary, with a deep-cycle battery and a charger that tests itself. It also covers a stuck primary switch. The alarm will tell you which one is running.

    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Revere

    Stuck floats and leaking check valves cause more sump failures than dead motors do. When the check valve leaks, the water in the pipe drops back into the basin and the pump has to move it twice, which shortens its life. New valve every time. In a tight pit we use a vertical switch, and in a wide pit a tethered float.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Revere, MA

    Pit filling, pump not running? Call (857) 335-4894 and we will send someone. Leave the lid off, get cords off the wet floor, and run a wet vac to keep the water level below the top of the pit while you wait. Common pumps ride on the truck, so most swaps happen right then. We will also figure out what killed the old pump.

    Pedestal Pumps for Shallow Pits in Revere, MA

    Older basements sometimes have a narrow, shallow pit poured right into the floor, and a pedestal pump is often the only thing that fits without breaking concrete. We swap the pump, replace the float rod and stops, and run new 1.5-inch PVC to the check valve. They usually cost less than a submersible.

    Sizing a Submersible Sump Pump in Revere

    Horsepower matters less than people think. For most houses a 1/3 HP pump is enough, provided the water only has to climb 10 to 15 feet to get out. Before we choose, we read the pump curve at your real discharge height. A pump that is too big empties the pit too fast and wears itself out.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Revere

    Basement bathrooms below the sewer line drain into a sealed, vented basin, and the ejector pump in that basin has to pass 2-inch solids. It is a different pump entirely. We replace the ejector and its discharge check valve, reseal the lid gasket, and confirm the vent ties in. A bad seal shows up as sewer odor.

    Zoeller Switch and Pump Repair in Revere

    Zoeller units with a piggyback float plug the pump cord into the switch cord, and if that piggyback plug fails the pump never gets power. That is the first thing we test. Some calls only need a new switch, while others need the whole pump. We tell you which before we sell you anything.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Revere

    A standard swap usually runs $500 to $1,200 installed. Add $800 to $1,800 for a battery backup system. Ejector pumps cost more, usually $1,200 to $2,500, since the basin is sealed and has to be vented.

    Like-for-like pump swaps usually do not need a permit in most jurisdictions. Adding a circuit or moving the discharge line can require one, and every city handles it a little differently. We check the local rule before we start.

    Every three months, and right before the wet season. Fill the pit with a bucket until the float lifts. The pump should start, empty the pit to the shutoff level and then stop on its own without humming or chattering.

    Two to four hours covers most standard swaps. Cutting in a new basin or running a new discharge line outside can push the job to a full day. We will tell you which kind of job it is before we start.

    Plan on 7 to 10 years from a decent pump. Pumps that sit dry for months can fail sooner, because the seal dries out and the float switch sticks in the off position. A quick bucket test every quarter catches it.

    Zoeller, Liberty Pumps, Wayne and Little Giant cover most of what we put in. For a primary pump we usually recommend cast iron. If you have a brand preference, we can work with it, as long as the pump is rated for your lift height and pit size.

    Budget a morning or an afternoon. The work includes pulling the old pump, cleaning the basin, installing the new pump and check valve, and running a fill test. Basin or discharge work adds time.

    Listen for grinding or rattling, and watch for a pump that cycles every minute or fails to start in heavy rain. Corrosion on the housing points the same way. Past 7 to 10 years, replace it before storm season.
