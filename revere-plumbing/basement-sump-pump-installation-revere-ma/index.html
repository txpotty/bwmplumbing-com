    Sump Pit Installation in Revere, MA

    Pit size controls how often the pump starts. A small 15 inch pit makes the pump cycle every minute in heavy flow, so we usually install an 18 by 22 inch or 18 by 30 inch basin with a solid bottom and perforated sides wrapped in filter fabric. Fewer starts mean a longer life.

    Primary Sump Pump Replacement in Revere

    Pumps wear out, usually somewhere between seven and ten years. We pull the old unit and inspect the pit, the check valve and the float clearance, then install a new pump on fresh PVC so you are not hanging a new motor on an old cracked fitting in Revere. Most swaps take two hours.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Revere, MA

    Submersible units sit down in the pit, sealed, with the water around them cooling the motor. You hear far less of them than a pedestal, and in Revere, MA we set them on a couple of bricks so the intake stays above the silt that settles on the bottom of every liner. The cord seal matters.

    Sump Basin and Discharge Line Installation in Revere

    The basin sets the ceiling on what the pump can do. We place it at the lowest point of the floor, bed it in washed stone, and run the discharge line with a slight pitch to the exit point so nothing sits in the pipe in Revere. Water should never come back.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Revere

    No pit yet? We find the low spot where water collects, saw cut the slab, dig roughly 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a liner surrounded by washed gravel so water can enter while fine soil stays out. We pour new concrete flush with the floor.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Revere

    Outages and heavy rain tend to show up together. Without house power, a battery backup keeps a second pump going, and a fully charged 75 amp-hour battery can run a typical backup pump for several hours of on-and-off cycling, longer on a light night. We load test the battery.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Revere, MA

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. Coastal communities also deal with storm surge and tidal flooding, which can compromise drains and septic systems near the water. A second pump goes into the same pit on its own float switch, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak. Your primary pump still handles the everyday load.

The concrete patch ends up flush with the floor.
