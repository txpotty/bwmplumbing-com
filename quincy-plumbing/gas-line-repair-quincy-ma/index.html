    Propane Gas Line Service in Quincy, MA

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. Our work covers propane yard lines, regulator replacement and fitting repairs, plus a check that every appliance has its propane orifices installed. Wrong orifices make a burner run dirty and unsafe. We test the system the same way we test natural gas piping.

    Emergency Gas Line Repair in Quincy

    If you smell gas, leave the building, skip the light switches and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Then call us.     Gas Appliance Connector Repair in Quincy, MA

    Weak flames or a smell near one appliance often point to the connector or shutoff behind it. Uncoated brass connectors from decades ago crack at the flare fittings and should be replaced on sight. We replace them with coated stainless connectors. We also add a reachable shutoff and a drip leg where needed, and confirm the new connector is rated for that appliance's BTU input.

    Gas Line Extensions and Relocations in Quincy

    Adding a gas fireplace, moving a laundry room or switching a range from electric to gas usually means extending the gas line. Sizing comes first. Our plumbers total the BTU load, measure the run and size the pipe so the last appliance on the line still gets full pressure at peak use. Then we route it, tie it in and test it.

    Gas Line Relocation and Upgrades in Quincy, MA

    A kitchen remodel or a new tankless water heater can push a gas system past what its original pipe can deliver. Tankless units need a lot of gas. A whole-house tankless heater can draw 150,000 BTU or more, and that often means upsizing the gas line all the way back toward the meter. We calculate the full load first. The new pipe gets sized so every burner in the house still has full pressure at once.

    Natural Gas Leak Detection and Repair in Quincy

    Finding a gas leak starts at the meter. With every appliance off, a moving test dial on the meter tells us gas is escaping somewhere, and then we isolate branches to narrow it down. An electronic detector does the rest. Soap solution at the suspect fitting confirms it with bubbles, and then we repair the joint, replace the section or swap the connector before testing again.
