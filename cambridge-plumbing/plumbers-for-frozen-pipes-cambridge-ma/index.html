    Attic and Exterior Wall Pipe Repair in Cambridge

    Many homes run supply lines through attics or crawlspaces to reach bathrooms and kitchens. Those are the first to freeze. After the repair, we either move the line, bury it under insulation on the warm side, or add heat cable.

    Burst Pipe Repair and Restoration in Cambridge, MA

    A burst pipe soaks drywall, insulation and flooring fast. We stop the water and repair the pipe. Once the line is fixed and tested, we can refer a water mitigation company to dry the space, and we leave you photos and an invoice describing the repair for your insurance claim.

    Pipe Insulation and Heat Cable Installation in Cambridge, MA

    Insulation slows heat loss, but it does not add heat. Exposed runs get heat cable. Self-regulating cable on a GFCI outlet, covered with insulation, keeps the pipe above freezing.

    Thawing Drain Lines and Sewer Pipes in Cambridge

    Drain lines can freeze too, mostly in crawlspaces and outside walls. You will see the backup first. We thaw drains with a steam or hot-water jetter fed through the cleanout, then check the slope, because a drain that holds water in a sag is the kind that freezes again next time.

    Outdoor Faucet and Sprinkler Line Freezing in Cambridge, MA

    Outdoor plumbing is the first to freeze, especially a spigot with a hose still attached. A connected hose holds water in the stem. Irrigation backflow devices sit fully exposed, so we drain or cover them before winter and repair split ones.

    Freeze Protection for Commercial Buildings in Cambridge, MA

    Vacant suites and warehouses freeze when the heat gets turned down too far. We set up freeze plans. That plan lists the shutoffs, the at-risk runs, the heat cable and the minimum thermostat setting for each space, so building staff know exactly what to do when a cold front is in the forecast.

    Slab Leak Detection for Frozen Pipes in Cambridge

    Pipes under a slab rarely freeze, but the lines where they rise out of the slab at an exterior wall can. That riser is the weak spot. Acoustic and thermal tools find slab-area leaks without breaking up the floor first.
