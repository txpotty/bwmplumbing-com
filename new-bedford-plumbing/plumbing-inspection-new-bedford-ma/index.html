    Pipe Material and Water Quality Review in New Bedford, MA

    Pipe material says a lot about what to expect from a house. We identify copper, CPVC, PEX, galvanized, polybutylene and Kitec, and we note the known failure patterns for each one. Certain types need a plan. Polybutylene and Kitec are known for sudden failures, and galvanized slowly closes up with rust, so those get flagged with replacement options.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in New Bedford, MA

    Many older homes have clay, cast iron or Orangeburg sewer lines that can crack, sag or fill with roots. We run a high-resolution camera from a cleanout to the city connection or septic tank. Every offset, belly, root intrusion and break shows up with its depth and location, and the recording is yours to keep.

    Fixture, Drain and Shutoff Valve Check in New Bedford, MA

    We run every faucet, shower and toilet and look for slow drains, leaks and weak flow. We test each valve. Stops and the main valve tend to seize with age, and gurgling drains can mean a vent problem, both of which are simple fixes once found.

    Water Heater Inspections in New Bedford

    Water heaters give clues long before they fail. We read the serial number for age, look for rust at the fittings and the base, check the venting and test the T and P relief valve. Tankless units get checked for scale. Gas heaters get a look at the flame, the drip leg and the vent draft, since a poorly venting heater is a safety issue.

    Residential Plumbing Inspections in New Bedford

    A full home inspection covers the system from the water meter to the sewer connection. We check every visible pipe for corrosion and leaks, run each fixture for flow and drainage and test the water heater, the main shutoff and the angle stops. Pressure gets measured too. We note anything above 80 psi.

    Gas Connection Safety Check in New Bedford, MA

    During a full inspection, we look at the visible gas piping and the connectors to each appliance. We check for old uncoated brass connectors, missing shutoffs, missing sediment traps and corrosion, and we test joints with an electronic detector. Leaks get reported immediately. Older homes are where we find the most issues, usually at appliance connectors that have been there since the house was built.
