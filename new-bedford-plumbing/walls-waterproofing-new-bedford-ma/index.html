    Foundation, Shower and Block Wall Sealing in New Bedford, MA

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in New Bedford

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, cement board with a membrane or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in New Bedford, MA

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the waterline and check it a day later. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a two-piece clamping drain set on the liner. The new pan gets its own flood test before tile.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in New Bedford

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. Long, cold heating seasons are the main stress on plumbing in Massachusetts, with months of near-freezing water entering the home. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in New Bedford

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. Nearly every older home has a basement, so foundation drainage and sump pumps matter for keeping the lower level dry. Backfill goes in last.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in New Bedford

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Most vertical cracks get sealed in one visit.

    Basement Wall Sealing in New Bedford, MA

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Long, cold heating seasons are the main stress on plumbing in Massachusetts, with months of near-freezing water entering the home. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then seal the face with a masonry waterproofer that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. The musty smell usually fades within weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in New Bedford, MA

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer with a line to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. The wall behind them can take a moisture-resistant board and sealer.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in New Bedford, MA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Renovations frequently uncover decades of patchwork plumbing, so repiping often makes more sense than repairing one section at a time. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with a new interior drain. Old mortar joints get repointed first.
