    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Everett, MA

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Everett

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. Humid summers bring condensation on cold water lines and musty basements, which can hide slow leaks until damage appears. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Everett

    Renovations frequently uncover generations of patchwork plumbing, so repiping often makes more sense than repairing one section at a time. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with real hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and install drain tile where water pools along the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Some basements never need a sump.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Everett, MA

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Humid summers bring condensation on cold water lines and damp basements, which can hide slow leaks until damage appears. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then seal the face with a masonry waterproofer that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. Most homeowners notice the musty smell is gone within a few weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Everett, MA

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer piped to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. The wall behind them can take a moisture-resistant board and sealer.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Everett, MA

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and run it to a sump basin so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. It just stops puddling on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Everett, MA

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Everett

    Crack injection runs about $300 to $800 per crack. Access changes the price a lot. We itemize every quote after the inspection.

    Schluter Kerdi, Custom RedGard and Laticrete Hydro Ban cover most of our showers. Sheet membranes like Kerdi suit a fast schedule since there is no cure time, while liquids like RedGard and Hydro Ban go on in two coats and need to dry between them. We follow each maker's steps exactly.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide because soil movement, the water table and the quality of the original install all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Over time, drain tile can fill with silt.

    Bubbling paint is a big one. Soft drywall near the floor, stained baseboards, a musty smell and white crust on block or stucco all point to moisture, and in a shower, loose tiles or cracked grout usually mean water is already behind the wall. We can confirm it with a meter in a few minutes.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, including the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Bigger foundation jobs take several days, sometimes more if it rains.

    Every install gets a written warranty on materials and workmanship. The coverage and the term are spelled out in your proposal, and when a manufacturer offers its own warranty on a membrane or resin we register it in your name. Keep the paperwork with your house records.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, heavy equipment, correct product thickness and an understanding of how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. A bad install can trap water against the wall and make things worse.
