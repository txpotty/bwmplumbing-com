    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Everett

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet usually costs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout usually runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. After-hours calls cost more.

    Rooting is safe with a trained tech and the correct cable and blade. Collapsed or badly cracked pipe can be hurt by anything run through it. Feel matters. If the cable tells us the pipe is damaged, we stop and send in a camera before going further.

    Most single-drain clogs take 1 to 3 hours from arrival to flow test. Main lines with heavy roots, or jobs that need a jetting pass, can stretch to half a day or more. Access is the wild card. With no usable cleanout, we pull a toilet to get in, and that adds about an hour.

    No permit is needed for routine rooting. A new cleanout or a pipe repair may need one. We pull permits for any repair work we do.

    A limited guarantee for a set number of days comes with most rooter work. A repeat clog in that window means a free return visit.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. Hair, wipes and roots respond well to it. Hydro jetting washes the entire inside of the pipe with high-pressure water, removing grease, scale and sludge the cable leaves on the walls. Repeat clogs usually need jetting.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. Repeated use can weaken old PVC joints and pit cast iron. They are dangerous to handle. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Everett's older homes take the most damage. They also make our job riskier. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.
