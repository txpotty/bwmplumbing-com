    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Everett

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. A large interceptor with a hardened grease cap can run past $1,000. The biggest variable is the gallons hauled, because disposal facilities charge by volume and a 1,500-gallon tank is a full truckload.

    Rules come from your sewer district or city pretreatment program. Most follow some version of the 25 percent rule. Plenty also cap the interval, want a year or more of records on site, and ban enzymes in place of pumping, so we look up your program before we schedule.

    Slow sinks come first. Then gurgling floor drains and a sour smell near the dish area. By the time gray water is coming up through a floor sink, the trap is usually well past 25 percent full and grease is already heading down the line.

    Staff can clean a small under-sink trap between pump-outs. Disposal still has to be legal. Many kitchens have staff skim daily and book us to fully pump and scrape every few weeks, which keeps the interval long without skipping the records the inspector expects.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. Haulers call it brown grease. Yellow grease, the used fryer oil in your collection bin, is a separate product that gets recycled and should never go into the trap.

    Short term, slow drains and smell. Longer term, a hardened grease cap that can take hours to break up. The hydrogen sulfide from rotting grease eats concrete too, and a neglected interceptor can need new baffles and risers years early.

    Grease passes the outlet and plugs the line. Backups follow, usually at the worst hour of service. Beyond the mess, many sewer districts fine kitchens for grease discharges and can bill them for jetting the public main, and a health inspector can close a kitchen with sewage on the floor.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. Automatic grease removal units are on the list too. Those AGRUs have a heater and a skimmer wheel that pull grease into a container, and they need the container emptied and the wheel and screen cleaned on a schedule.
