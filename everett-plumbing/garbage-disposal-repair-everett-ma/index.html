    Noise and Odor Diagnosis in Everett

    A rattle that stops after a few seconds is usually a spoon or a pit bouncing in the chamber. A steady whine is different. A steady whine usually points to failing motor bearings, and the noise keeps building until the motor locks up. Odors usually trace to the splash guard. The guard comes out, the chamber gets cleaned and a torn guard gets replaced.

    Disposal Odor and Buildup Cleaning in Everett, MA

    Food buildup and grease on the chamber walls and under the splash guard are behind most disposal odors. Pinhole leaks in older copper lines are a familiar problem where the water is corrosive, and they tend to show up in clusters. Scale gives that food something to stick to. Cleaning the guard and chamber, checking the drain and swapping a torn guard often solves a smell that lemon peels never fixed.

    Disposal Repair vs Replacement Costs in Everett, MA

    Most jams and small leaks are much cheaper than a replacement. Once a unit is eight to ten years old, leaking from the body or losing its motor, replacement usually wins, and we price both when it is a close call. You make the call.

    Grinding Noise Repair in Everett

    Loud metal-on-metal sounds may mean a loose mount, a foreign object or worn impellers. We inspect the chamber, tighten the mounting ring and remove any debris, then run the unit empty to hear whether the motor itself is making the noise. Bad bearings mean replacement.

    Jam Clearing and Motor Repair in Everett

    Bones, fruit pits and bottle caps lodge between the grind ring and the impeller lugs, and the motor stalls. We shut off power, insert a 1/4 inch hex key into the socket on the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until the flywheel turns freely. Next we pull the object with pliers. Long stalls can overheat the motor, so we run it under load before we finish.

    Noisy Disposal Repair in Everett

    Grinding, rattling or a hum that sounds rough all point to something inside. Loose mounting rings rattle too. We check the mount, then look for debris, then run the motor empty, since a whine with an empty chamber means the bearings are going.
