    Frequently Asked Questions About Bathtub Faucet Replacement in Framingham

    Most tub faucet replacements run $250 to $600 with a standard faucet. A trim and cartridge swap on a valve that stays in the wall sits near the low end, while replacing the valve itself, especially behind tile with no access panel, lands near the top. The price is set before work starts.

    A worn cartridge or stem washer is the usual cause. On a single-handle valve the seals inside the cartridge have hardened or cracked, and on a two- or three-handle faucet the washer and the brass seat under it are worn, so water slips past even with the handle closed. New parts stop it.

    Yes, usually in under an hour. We check whether the spout is a slip-on with a set screw or a threaded type, match the length of the pipe sticking out of the wall, and pick a diverter spout if you have a shower above. We caulk the top and sides only.

    We try to. Most faucet makers sell trim in chrome, brushed nickel, matte black and oil-rubbed bronze, and we can usually match the tub trim to your sink faucet and towel bars from the same brand line. Brushed finishes vary slightly.

    Wall-mount valves with a spout are most common. Deck-mount fillers sit on the tub rim or surround, freestanding fillers rise from the floor beside the tub, and many valves combine a tub spout with a shower head through a diverter. Each has its own rough-in.

    The diverter is wearing out. On a spout diverter the rubber gate inside no longer seals, and on a three-handle faucet the middle stem washer has worn, so water splits between the spout and the shower head instead of going all to one. A new diverter spout costs little.

    Moen, Delta, Kohler and American Standard cover most of our work. Pfister, Grohe, Hansgrohe and Symmons are common too, and designer brands work if the specs are published. Parts support matters more than looks.
