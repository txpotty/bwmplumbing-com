    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Haverhill, MA

    Switching from a tank is more than a swap. A typical 40-gallon gas tank uses around 40,000 BTU, while a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the gas line from the meter almost always gets upsized. Venting changes too. We plan both on the quote.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Haverhill

    Sizing a home unit comes down to two numbers: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. With a shower at about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen tap a bit under, two showers and a sink together need around 5 or 6 gallons per minute at the needed rise. It shortens the list quickly.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Haverhill, MA

    Calcium scale is what kills most tankless units early. Much of the public water in Massachusetts is soft and slightly acidic, which is easy on fixtures but can gradually wear through copper pipe. Scale coats the heat exchanger, cuts efficiency and triggers error codes, and left alone it can crack the exchanger and end the warranty claim before it starts. Flush it yearly. Service valves make it easy.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Haverhill

    Gas tankless units in Haverhill are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. Most homes need the line upsized from the meter, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or larger. We test the new gas piping before first fire.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Installation in Haverhill

    Commercial hot water loads in Haverhill are heavier and more constant than residential. A busy restaurant may run a dish machine, three hand sinks and a mop sink at once, and the tankless rack has to hold temperature through the whole lunch rush without dropping a degree. Units share a header manifold. One can be serviced while the rest run.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Haverhill, MA

    Replacing an old tankless unit tends to go faster than a conversion. Since the gas line and vent are already there, most swaps mean matching connections, inspecting the old vent and mounting the new heater. Vent sizes can change by brand. We check before ordering.
