    What a Plumbing Inspection Covers in Boston, MA

    Gas Connection Safety Check in Boston, MA

    During a full inspection, we look at the visible gas piping and the connectors to each appliance. We check for old uncoated brass connectors, missing shutoffs, missing sediment traps and corrosion, and we test joints with an electronic detector. Any leak is reported on the spot. Most of our gas findings are in older homes, often at original appliance connectors.

    Water Heater and Softener Inspection in Boston

    We check the water heater's age by serial number, look for rust, leaks and sediment and test the relief valve. Venting and gas connections get checked. We also look at the expansion tank, drain pan and softener, and a hardness test shows whether the softener is working or just eating salt.

    Hard Water Damage Assessment in Boston

    Much of the public water in Massachusetts is soft and slightly acidic, which is easy on fixtures but can gradually wear through copper pipe. Hard water leaves scale in pipes, fixtures and water heaters. Signs include crusted aerators, clogged showerheads, slow toilet fill and heater sediment, and we can test hardness during the visit. Then we explain your options. Softeners, filters and scale reducers all help, and we suggest the one that matches the reading we took.

    Residential Plumbing Inspections in Boston

    A full home inspection covers the system from the water meter to the sewer connection. Our plumber looks at all exposed pipe, runs every faucet and toilet and tests the heater, the main valve and the fixture stops. Pressure gets measured too. We note anything above 80 psi.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Boston, MA

    Many older homes have clay, cast iron or Orangeburg sewer lines that can crack, sag or fill with roots. We run a high-resolution camera from a cleanout to the city connection or septic tank. Every offset, belly, root intrusion and break shows up with its depth and location, and the recording is yours to keep.

    Rental and Commercial Property Inspections in Boston

    Landlords and property managers use our inspections between tenants or before buying an investment property. We document each unit. We note heaters, fixtures and sewers by unit, and commercial jobs can include backflow and grease lines, giving owners a budget for repairs.

    Sewer Line Camera Inspections in Boston, MA

    A sewer camera shows the inside of the main drain without digging.     Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Boston

    Standard plumbing inspections typically run $150 to $300. With the sewer camera, most totals land between $250 and $500.

    Most homes do well with an inspection every one to two years. Before buying is a good time too. If you are purchasing a property, live in an older house or have had recent leaks or backups, a yearly check gives you a better chance of catching the next problem early.

    Looking for drips and watching the meter is worth doing on your own. It just goes only so far. A professional inspection uses cameras, pressure gauges and leak detection tools to check the parts you cannot see, including the sewer line and the pipe inside walls, which is where the expensive problems usually hide.

    High water bills, repeat clogs, slow drains and discolored water are common signs. Banging pipes count too. Strange sounds from the pipes or the water heater, along with visible moisture or mold near fixtures, usually point to an underlying problem that needs a professional look before it gets worse.

    We can include estimated repair ranges in the report and firm quotes if you want us to do the work. There is no obligation to hire us for repairs.

    Our technicians inspect all major water heater brands, including Rheem, AO Smith and Bradford White. Tankless units are covered. Rinnai and Noritz tankless units and most household fixture brands are routine, so your equipment brand will not be a problem.

    No permit is needed for an inspection. Repairs may need one. Permits come into play for repairs, replacements or new installations that follow from the inspection, and if any of those apply, we will tell you up front and handle the paperwork if we do the work.

    You can do a basic check by looking under sinks and watching your water meter. A plumber goes further. A professional inspection adds sewer camera video, pressure readings, leak tracing and code knowledge, which together catch problems that a visual check simply cannot see.
