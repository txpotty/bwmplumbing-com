    CSST Repair in Boston

    Corrugated stainless steel tubing is a flexible gas pipe with a plastic jacket, often yellow, that runs through attics and walls in newer homes. It can be damaged. Common damage comes from nails, rodents or lightning, and repairs require cutting out the bad section and using fittings from the same manufacturer. Bonding gets checked on every system. The system should be bonded to the electrical grounding system with a proper clamp and conductor, per the manufacturer's instructions and local code.

    Gas Appliance Line Installation in Boston, MA

    A new range, dryer or water heater needs a gas line sized for its BTU rating and the length of the run. Undersized lines starve burners. We install the branch line, add an accessible shutoff valve and a sediment trap where the code calls for one, then connect the appliance with a new coated flexible connector. If the existing appliance line is leaking or damaged, we fix it on the same visit.

    Gas Leak Detection in Boston

    A rotten egg smell, hissing near a line, dead grass in a stripe across the yard or a gas meter that moves with everything off are the common leak signs. Trust your nose. We isolate sections of the system and use electronic combustible gas detectors to narrow the source, then confirm with soap solution at fittings, valves and unions. Once we find it, you get a written repair price before work starts.

    Emergency Gas Line Repair in Boston

    If you smell gas, leave the building, skip the light switches and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Then call us. After the gas company secures the meter, our plumber finds the leak, repairs it and runs the test the utility needs to see before turning gas back on. We relight pilots when service returns. Most emergency repairs are done in a single visit with parts from the truck.

    Gas Line Relocation and Upgrades in Boston, MA

    A kitchen remodel or a new tankless water heater can push a gas system past what its original pipe can deliver. Tankless units need a lot of gas. A whole-house tankless heater can draw 150,000 BTU or more, and that often means upsizing the gas line all the way back toward the meter. We calculate the full load first. The new pipe gets sized so every burner in the house still has full pressure at once.

    Corroded Gas Pipe Replacement in Boston

    Buried steel gas line corrodes once its coating cracks, and moisture, roots and ground movement speed it up. Tree roots and shifting fill around old clay or cast iron sewer laterals are a frequent cause of backups in established neighborhoods. Rusted buried steel gets replaced with polyethylene and tracer wire, and we use a listed transition riser where it rises to the house. Exposed sections get coated steel. We test before backfilling so any problem joint is fixed while the trench is still open.

    Black Iron Pipe Repair in Boston, MA

    Black iron is the standard interior gas pipe in a lot of homes, and it lasts a long time indoors. Joints are the weak point. Years of vibration from a furnace or a house settling can loosen threaded fittings, and pipe in a damp garage or an exterior wall can rust from the outside in. Bad sections get replaced with new pipe. Outdoor runs get coated or painted after the repair so rust does not start again.

    Appliance Connector and Shutoff Repair in Boston

    Old flexible connectors, especially uncoated brass ones, crack and leak with age. We replace them with new coated stainless connectors sized for the appliance and add an accessible shutoff valve if the old one is missing or seized. This is a quick fix. Ranges, dryers, water heaters and furnaces all need proper connections, and most also need a sediment trap ahead of the appliance gas valve.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Repair in Boston

    Small fitting or connector repairs typically cost $150 to $450. Section replacements run $400 to $1,200, and buried line replacement can reach $1,000 to $4,000 or more.

    Gas lines do not need routine service the way a water heater does. A visual check helps, though. During other plumbing visits, we can look over visible gas piping, connectors and shutoffs and point out old brass connectors, missing sediment traps or rust that is worth fixing before it leaks.

    CSST is approved and widely used when installed and bonded correctly. We verify bonding and repair damaged sections with manufacturer-approved parts.

    Leaks at the meter or on the utility's service line belong to the gas company. Do not try to fix it yourself. Get out of the house, call the utility's emergency line, and once they have handled their side, we can repair any leaks on the house side of the meter, including the riser and the yard line.

    Our gas line repairs come with a written warranty on parts and labor, usually one year. Coverage varies by repair. The exact terms are listed on your estimate before work starts, and any new connectors, valves or CSST fittings also carry their manufacturer warranties.

    Tightening a union or swapping a connector takes an hour or two. Buried lines take longer. A buried line replacement typically fills a full day once you add trenching, pipe, testing and backfill, and the inspection may add a little more.

    Leave the building right away and do not use switches or flames. Call your gas utility or 911 from outside, and once the utility makes it safe, call us for the repair.

    We work on black iron, coated steel, CSST such as Gastite and TracPipe, and polyethylene for buried lines. Each material has its place. Polyethylene can only run underground and needs a listed riser where it comes up, while CSST needs bonding and black iron needs proper support and gas-rated thread sealant.
