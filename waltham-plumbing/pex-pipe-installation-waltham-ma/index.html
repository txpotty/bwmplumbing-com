    Leak Repair with PEX in Waltham, MA

    A single bad section does not always mean a repipe.     PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Waltham, MA

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. All three are code-listed for drinking water. We match the pipe to the fittings and tools from one maker so the warranty stays intact.

    Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Waltham, MA

    Think of a manifold like an electrical panel, but for water lines. A central header feeds each fixture on its own line with its own valve, which lets you shut off one toilet and leave the kitchen running. It uses more pipe. The trade-off is almost no fittings hidden in the walls and steadier pressure when two showers run at once.

    Whole-Home PEX Repiping in Waltham

    Low pressure at every faucet, across the whole house, usually points at the pipe and not the fixtures. In an older house that usually means rust-choked galvanized or polybutylene with tired fittings, and a new faucet cartridge will not touch it. Repiping does fix it. We replace every supply line from the main valve to each angle stop and test the system before closing anything.

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Waltham, MA

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to replace old supply lines. While the studs are exposed we can run PEX back to a manifold or a sound trunk, set fresh stub-outs for the sink and dishwasher, and add a fridge line. An addition gets sized on its own. A far-end bathroom may need a three-quarter inch feed to hold pressure.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Waltham

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Either one lasts for decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.
