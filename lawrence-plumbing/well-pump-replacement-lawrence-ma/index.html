    Pump and Pressure Tank Matching in Lawrence

    Pump and tank work as a pair. An undersized or waterlogged tank forces dozens of extra starts a day, and each start puts heat into the motor windings. Every pump job includes a tank check.

    Control Box and Pressure Switch Replacement in Lawrence, MA

    Not every dry faucet means a dead pump. Pressure switches with burned contacts or a clogged pickup tube can leave the pump sitting idle while the tank drains to zero. The switch itself is a cheap part. We show you the burned contacts.

    Well Pump Sizing in Lawrence

    Sizing starts with the well log. The log, or a sounder down the casing, gives us static water level and well depth, and a draw-down test tells us roughly how many gallons per minute the well can actually give. Pump more than the well yields and it runs dry.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Lawrence, MA

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. Air sneaks in through a pinhole in the suction line, the pump loses prime and the seal burns up running dry. The suction line gets a pressure test. If the well is too deep for a jet pump, we will say so and quote a submersible.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Lawrence

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. If the pump clicks on every time someone runs a faucet for ten seconds, the bladder in the tank has probably failed and the pump is short cycling itself to death. We check the air charge with a gauge. Precharge should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Lawrence

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Most fail from sand chewing up the impellers or a lightning surge that cooks the windings. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. The reading shows whether the fault is the motor or the wire.
