    Well Pump Problems We Fix in Lawrence, MA

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Lawrence

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. BWM Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Lawrence, MA well. Usually the electronics are fine.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Lawrence, MA

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Air leaks on the suction side cause most failures. BWM Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. Nonstop running without pressure usually means lost prime or an air leak.

    Above-Ground Jet Pump Repair in Lawrence

    Lost prime is the number one jet pump complaint. These pumps can also leak at the shaft seal or wear out an impeller. We reprime the pump, replace the seal and check the foot valve and suction line for leaks, since one tiny air leak will undo the whole repair. Everything is above ground.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Lawrence

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up instead of pulling it, and they run quiet for years, but motors, impellers and wire splices still fail eventually. We pull the pump with the right equipment, test it and replace only what failed. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Lawrence, MA well.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Lawrence, MA

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Coastal communities also deal with storm surge and tidal flooding, which can compromise drains and septic systems near the water. Lightning and power surges damage those parts more than anything else. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one. An arrestor costs little.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Lawrence

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.

    Low Water Pressure Repair in Lawrence

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Control Box & Electrical Repair in Lawrence, MA

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. A tripped breaker is often the first sign. Licensed technicians test these parts safely with the power locked out, replace what failed and tighten every connection in the box. Wiring meets Massachusetts code when the job is done.
