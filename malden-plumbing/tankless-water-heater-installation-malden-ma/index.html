    What Goes Into a Tankless Install in Malden, MA

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Malden

    Conversions in Malden usually take a full day. The old tank gets drained and hauled off, the gas line is upsized and tested, a new vent goes out through a wall or roof, and the unit is mounted, plumbed with isolation valves and fired. You shower that night. Inspection follows within days.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Malden, MA

    Calcium scale is the most common reason tankless heaters fail. Pinhole leaks in older copper lines are a familiar problem where the water is aggressive, and they tend to show up in clusters. Once scale coats the heat exchanger, efficiency drops and error codes start, and over time it can crack the exchanger and void the warranty. A yearly flush prevents it. Service valves make it easy.

    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Malden

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, since the burner fires only after it senses flow. A recirc pump on a timer or sensor keeps the hot line primed, cutting the wait at the far sink from about a minute to a few seconds. Less water goes down the drain.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Installation in Malden

    Commercial hot water loads in Malden run longer and harder than a house. A busy restaurant may run a dish machine, three hand sinks and a mop sink at once, and the tankless rack has to hold temperature through the whole lunch rush without dropping a degree. We rack units with a common manifold. One can be serviced while the rest run.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Malden, MA

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Malden, MA homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. Panel capacity gets checked up front.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Malden

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and several models come with a recirculation pump built in. That pump can push hot water toward a far bathroom on a schedule, which shortens the wait at the tap without adding a separate pump under the sink. Venting is usually two-inch or three-inch PVC. Condensing units also need a condensate drain.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Malden

    Gas tankless units in Malden are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We test the new gas piping before first fire.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Malden, MA

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and parts are easy to get. We install both condensing and non-condensing Rinnai models, and the right one depends on where the vent can go and how much you want to spend up front. The non-condensing ones need stainless vent pipe. Condensing models use PVC vent and a condensate drain.
